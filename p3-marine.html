P3 MarineFrom spreadsheets and chats to one platform

P3 Marine is an expense management and reporting platform for family offices and companies that manage high value assets, above all yachts: expenses, invoices, prepaid crew cards, virtual IBANs in three currencies and a CRM for everyone around a yacht, from owners to captains, crew and charter clients. I designed it from zero at BARVA.TECH with a team of 20+: 300+ screens, five breakpoints and an interface for each role. Here is only a small part: the project is large, with complex documentation, logic and adaptation for every screen.

01The problem

Yachts worth $50M, run on spreadsheets and group chats

Yacht management companies ran millions in yearly costs through spreadsheets, PDFs, group chats and phone calls. The software that existed was generic and assumed nobody needed it at sea.

Every report had to serve people with very different authority, from an owner who signs off big spending to a crew member logging a taxi fare. And the crew often had no reliable connection at all.

02Roles

The hardest problem wasn't the interface. It was the roles.

Five roles work in the platform every day, and charter clients get a read only view. Each role has its own dashboard and its own information architecture, with no greyed out features.

Owners and crew don't need different permissions. They need different products.

An owner wants one number and a decision to make. A crew member wants a button that works on deck. Treating them as the same user with fewer rights would have failed both, so each role got its own information hierarchy.

03Research and structure

Mapping the domain before drawing a screen

We started with how management companies actually work, profiled every role and compared Yachtspot, Dockmaster and Siren. They all assumed users were maritime experts, and that was the gap.

Then I spent two weeks on an entity model: yachts, owners, crew, cards, charters, documents and how they connect. Every screen after that had a place to live. Features were ranked by impact and effort, and validation and error messages went through three redesigns after testing.

04Onboarding

From sign up to an active company in eight clear steps

A management company signs up, verifies email and phone, turns on two factor authentication and creates its company. Clients of P3 Bank connect their bank account right away.

The last screen doesn't just say “wait”. It shows what is done, what is in progress and what comes next, so nobody has to email support to ask where their account is.

07Crew cards and payments

A prepaid card instead of a cash envelope

Crews used to get cash in an envelope and a pile of receipts back. Now managers issue, top up and block prepaid cards from the platform, and the crew logs every expense from their phone. At sea it keeps working: expenses wait in a queue and sync when the connection comes back.

11Reflection

What I would do differently

We underinvested in the owner's view. The people with the most authority got the least design attention, because they used the platform the least.

Next time I would start with a simple summary layer for owners: what the yacht cost this month and what needs their decision. One clear picture would have been worth more to them than any table.
