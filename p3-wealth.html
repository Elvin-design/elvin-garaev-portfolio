P3 WealthA capital management platform, built from zero

P3 Wealth is a regulated platform where professional investors manage the capital of wealthy clients: they invest it in BlackRock funds and other assets, track every position and report back, and the platform earns a share of the profit. I designed the whole product from zero: the structure, the design system, dashboards and charts, onboarding, roles, statements and the documentation developers built from.

01The brief

A regulated business that needed a product

The business model, the regulation and the BlackRock data were there. The product that professional investors would actually use every day was not. My job was to design it: keep a regulated system exactly as strict, and make it feel simple for people who move millions.

1Institutions with many signatoriesOpening an account meant forms, documents and sign off from several people.
2Trust at this level of moneyInvestors expect security, transparency and clarity on every screen.
3Roles in a regulated worldInvestors, managers, admins and compliance see very different things.
4Many systems, one interfacePayments, fund data, reporting and compliance had to feel like one product.

Professional investors don't trust what they don't understand.

So clarity became part of compliance: every number with its source, every action with its consequence, and nothing that makes an investor reach for the phone. And nothing that forces them to finish everything in one go.

03Design system

A design system built for numbers

I built the design system from scratch: color and type tokens, inputs with every validation state, buttons, dropdowns, phone and currency fields, tables and chart elements. Error messages were written to guide, not to alarm, because a professional investor who feels lost stops trusting the product.

Every component came with documentation, so developers could build screens from the system without guessing.

P3 Wealth · Design system

Navy for structure, rose gold for action, whites in five grades, Simplon Norm for text and Simplon Mono for figures. Redrawn from the original system.

Numbers you can scan

Every figure in tables uses a monospaced font, Simplon Mono, so digits line up in columns. In testing it cut read errors by 23%.

04Dashboards and charts

Tables and charts you can read at a glance

Investors hold funds in euros, dollars and pounds, so the currency became a switch in the header: one tap, and every balance, table and chart updates. You can try it in the dashboard at the top of this page.

The performance chart went through three iterations. Tabs made people unsure whether a click would take them to another page, so the final version toggles between AUM and yield with a clear active state. Every BlackRock number carries its source and update date.

Every screen on three widths

Investors check the dashboard on a large monitor in the office and on a tablet between meetings. Each screen was designed for 1440, 1024 and 768, with tables that keep their meaning instead of just getting smaller.

05Onboarding

Save and continue, on any device

Professional investors open accounts between meetings, not in one free afternoon. So we added Save and continue: the investor saves their progress, gets a secure link by email and picks up on any device, at the same step, with every answer in place.

Approvals stopped blocking the investor too. Compliance, BlackRock and cosigners get their requests at the same time and sign off in parallel. Now an account started in the morning is ready by the evening, not the next day.

06Accounts and paperwork

Signatories, access and forms inside the account

An institutional account has authorised signatories, authorised dealers and people who only need to look. The profile shows who is who and when each person accepted the invitation, and an investor can invite a colleague with read only access in a minute.

The paperwork moved inside too. BlackRock's account amendment form for the Institutional Cash Series became a guided web form in the platform instead of a PDF sent back and forth by email.

07Roles and security

Four roles, four interfaces

Investors, fund managers, administrators and compliance officers use the same platform for very different jobs. Each role gets its own interface, not a locked version of someone else's. What a role doesn't need is hidden, not disabled, so nobody ever lands on an access denied screen.

09Reflection

What I learned

Building a regulated product from zero taught me that clarity is part of compliance. When professional investors understand every number, they trust the platform, and they stop calling someone to explain it.

The fastest onboarding is one you can pause. Save and continue and parallel approvals turned two working days into one, because they fit how professionals actually work: between meetings, on different devices.

If I started again, I would bring compliance officers into research as early as investors. Much of the waiting came from approvals, and they knew best where it could be removed.
