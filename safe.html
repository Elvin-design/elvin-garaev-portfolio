Banking SafeSaving with a name, a goal and a date

Ukrsib Online already had deposits and savings accounts, but people saw them as bank products, not as their own plans. Safe turns saving into named goals with visible progress: a car, a trip or a child's education, each with its own amount, date and regular top ups. As the senior product designer I worked with marketing, developers and the bank's stakeholders through the whole cycle, from the first hypothesis to the final screens.

The bet

If saving feels personal and progress is visible, people save more and stay longer

Ukrsib Online has more than two million users, so even a small change in how people save affects the bank's liquidity and how long clients stay. Safe was a strategic bet on that hypothesis, and the whole process was built to test it before a single production screen was approved.

01The problem

Savings that felt like paperwork

People understood how deposits and savings accounts worked, but did not connect them to anything in their own lives. A deposit was a rate and a term, not a holiday or a car. And the bank's goal pulled in a different direction from the user's.

1Products, not plansDeposits and accounts were described by rates and terms, not by goals.
2One pot for everythingPeople saved for several things at once in a single account.
3No sense of progressNothing showed how close a goal was, so saving felt endless.
4Two goals in tensionThe bank wanted money to stay longer, people wanted it within reach.
02Research

Seven steps from a business goal to a tested idea

Saving is personal and often irrational, so we did not guess. Each step of the research fed the next one, and every decision on the final screens can be traced back to it.

Align the business and the user

A kickoff mapped the bank's objectives against what makes people save. The key tension surfaced on the first day, and every later decision had to respect both sides.

The bankWants money to stay longer⇄PeopleWant their money within reach

Study the market, in Ukraine and abroad

Ukrainian banks treated savings as a financial instrument. International banks treated saving as a behaviour. Monobank's jar came closest, so we studied why it worked and where it fell short.

Ukrainian banksrates and termsMonobank's jarthe closest referenceInternational bankssaving as a habitThe gapexperience, not interest rates

Ask the right questions

We wrote more than 80 research questions and grouped them to find the unknowns that mattered most before talking to anyone.

80+questionsGoals and dreamsSaving habitsHow people track progressWhy saving stopsTrust in the bank

Listen to savers

Interviews with active and occasional savers showed the same pattern again and again: goals lived in people's heads, with no system to track them.

Active saverswho save regularlyOccasional saverswho start and stopThe patternseveral goals, one account

Turn findings into hypotheses

Every finding became a testable hypothesis, ranked by impact and feasibility. Workshops grouped them into themes.

Progressvisual and in percentReminderstied to milestonesTogethershared SafesAutomationregular top ups

Sketch before polishing

Hypotheses became a feature list and wireframes we could put in front of people quickly. Three competing approaches were compared before one was chosen.

Test, then map every state

Key decisions went through A/B tests, and a full user map covered every state, so no edge case was left undesigned.

11Outcome

What research and tests proved before launch

Visible progress brings people backIn the A/B test, the version with a progress bar got more return visits than the balance alone.
Less choice, faster startToo many options lowered adoption, so automatic top ups came down to every day, week or month.
A name makes it personalSafes with a name and a picture kept people much more engaged in testing.
Every state designedA full user map covered every screen and error, and the feature is now in final development.

The feature is in final development, so these are findings from research and A/B tests, not launch figures.

12Reflection

What I learned

Saving is emotional before it is financial. A name and a picture turned an account into a promise, and that did more for commitment than any rate on the screen.

Less choice made people act. Three schedule options worked better than a full calendar, and that lesson now shapes how I design every setting.

Next I want the launch numbers. The most interesting one will be how many Safes actually reach their goal.
