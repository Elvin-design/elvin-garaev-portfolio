01The problem

A bank run through personal phones and Excel

A client might call in the morning, email a document at lunch, write in the app chat in the evening and send a message on social media at night. Every channel had its own tool and its own queue, so managers had to stand guard in all of them at once, often with several clients waiting at the same time.

Many conversations moved to messengers on managers' own phones. Fast for the client, invisible for the bank, and a real compliance risk, because none of it was logged or encrypted.

Client history lived in Excel files and notebooks, with no templates and no shared statuses. The irony was hard to miss: the bank had great digital products for its clients, Ukrsib Online and Ukrsib Business, while the people serving those clients still worked like it was 2010.

02The idea

Every channel, one secure panel

Instead of asking clients to change their habits, we bring every channel to the manager. A call, an email, the app chat, the website or a social media message all land in one queue, next to the client's record, inside a channel the bank controls.

03Research

We went where the problem actually lived

Documented processes and real work almost never match. People build workarounds around broken systems, and that is where the real insights hide. So before any wireframe, I travelled to bank branches and watched managers handle real clients in real time.

  1. 01KyivHead office and branches
  2. 02LvivBranch visits
  3. 03KharkivBranch visits
  4. 04PoltavaBranch visits
  5. 05VinnytsiaBranch visits

We expected tool overload. We found anxiety.

Managers were not only juggling tasks. They were carrying relationships and urgency for 550+ clients with no system to help them prioritise. A manager in Kharkiv told us she woke up at 2am to check whether a client had replied. That changed the goal of the project: not another chat tool, but a system that gives managers back control of their day.

04Two users, one system

Same broken system, two very different pains

Like many B2B teams, we first focused only on the front line user and almost missed the second one. The persona work made us stop and design for both.

Premium banking manager
Measured by

A

Anna, 35

Measured by

Invisibility. No live view of workload or results, so she can't balance the load or see who deserves recognition. She hears about problems only when a client escalates.
Needs
One place with workload, statuses and results for every manager

The core challenge: two overlapping products on one data layer. Vasyl needs depth inside one conversation, Anna needs visibility across all of them, and neither view can clutter the other.

05Journeys

Four journeys, three failures, three principles

I mapped four real scenarios step by step, with the client's emotions, the manager's pain and every tool involved. The same three failures showed up in all of them, and they became the principles behind the product.

Data moves between systems by itself, so nobody retypes it from one tool into another.

06Key decisions

Six to eight tools became one. Here is how.

Client first, not chat first

A messenger style layout felt natural, but in testing managers kept jumping to SAP and lost the thread. They think in clients, not in conversations.

Three columns after the second round

Round one had two columns. Managers liked how clean it was, and still went back to SAP for account data. Density feels simple when everything on screen is about the same client.

07The product

One screen for the client, one for the team

Vasyl works in the three column workspace you saw at the top. Anna opens the sector overview: workload, results and the cases that need her today.

Seeing everything in one place lets her balance the load fairly and recognise the people who deliver. For a sector head, that is how a team stays motivated. Both views share the navigation and the app switcher with Ukrsib Business, so moving between bank tools feels like one product.

10Reflection

What I would do differently

The most valuable part of this project was the research, not the interface. It showed that a problem which looked like missing tooling was really a missing internal experience.

If I started again, I would give the client's side its own research thread. We designed deeply for managers, but the person receiving a message through the new system deserves the same attention.
