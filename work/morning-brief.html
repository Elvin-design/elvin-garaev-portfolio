Morning brief in SlackA different message for every finance role

A treasury platform helps mid-size companies pay and get paid in several currencies. In this project I designed one feature: a short Slack message at 7:30 that tells each role what needs them today, with up to three buttons to act on it, and the settings where a company decides what each role sees.

01The idea

Finance teams start the day in Slack, not in a dashboard

A treasury platform already knows a lot: cash in every bank, payment runs waiting for approval, currency exposure and policy limits. But every role needs a different part of it, and on most mornings the important part is one approval or one risk.

So the brief brings that part to where people already are, before the first meeting. Anything that does not need action today stays in the platform.

Approvals and risks

CFO

Cash against the safety buffer, the payment run waiting for approval and the limits that are close to a breach.

Currencies and hedges

Treasurer

Overnight currency moves, the dollars that are not hedged yet, and the hedges and transfers to make today.

Today's payments

Accounts payable

The payments that go out today, the ones flagged for a second look and the invoices on hold.

02The message

One shape for every brief, built around today

Every brief has the same five parts, so people learn it once and then just read. The order follows one question: what do I need to do today?

  1. 01SummaryOne line on what matters
  2. 02Three numbersEach compared with yesterday
  3. 03One chartLast 7 days or this week
  4. 04TodayThree things to act on
  5. 05ButtonsThree at most

Today first. Yesterday only as context.

Every number shows the change since yesterday, and every chart ends on today, highlighted. If something can wait, it is not in the brief. That keeps the message short enough to read in a minute.

03Roles

Same shape, a different brief for every role

The CFO gets a direct message about approvals and risks. The treasurer gets currency moves and the hedges to place. Accounts payable gets one message in their team channel about today's payment run. Each role has its own numbers, chart and buttons.

Companies, people and numbers are demo data.

04Settings

Each company decides what every role sees

The brief is set up in the platform. An admin adds roles and picks the time, the time zone, the days and where the brief goes: a direct message or a channel. For each role they turn sections on and off, drag them into order, and choose the chart and up to three buttons.

A live preview shows the exact Slack message before it goes out, and Send test to me sends it to yourself first.

Click to zoom

Settings for the CFO brief, with a live preview. Demo data.

Buttons

Three at most

A message is not a menu. The limit keeps every brief about the next step.

Alerts

Only two right away

Cash below the buffer and a big currency move send a message at once. Everything else waits for the morning.

Personal

Hide it for yourself

People can hide a section in Slack without changing the brief for the whole role.

05AI

AI writes the words, the platform gives the numbers

The summary line and the Today list are written by AI from the platform data, in plain language and in the order things are due.

The numbers, the chart and the buttons come straight from the platform, so the AI never makes up a figure. Every Today item opens its source in the platform, and Ask a question answers only from the company's own data.

Written by AI

The words

The greeting, the one line summary and the three things to do today.

From the platform

The facts

Every number, the chart, the buttons and the link behind each item.

06Next

How I would know it works

The feature has not launched yet, so there are no results to show. After launch I would watch three signals.

Signal 1

Read before 9:00

How many briefs people open before the first meeting of the day.

Signal 2

Done from Slack

How many approvals and reviews start from the buttons and not from the dashboard.

Signal 3

Sections hidden

What people hide for themselves shows what is noise for each role.
