01The problem

Crypto wallets were built for experts

In 2021 we audited the wallets people used most. The same problems kept coming up: seed phrases with no explanation, token amounts instead of money, no real onboarding and screens that looked like spreadsheets.

For anyone who wasn't a crypto person, the scariest moment was sending money. You paste a 42 character address, check it twice and still hope. One wrong character and the money is gone for good.

And the safest way to hold crypto, a cold wallet, was also the least friendly. XGo had to be both: as safe as cold storage and as easy as a bank app.

People weren't afraid of crypto. They were afraid of mistakes they couldn't undo.

Every decision after that answered one question: how do we make the safe choice the easy one? Cold storage by default, names instead of addresses and money shown as money.

03Cold storage

Savings in a vault, spending on a card

A cold wallet keeps keys offline. It is the safest way to hold crypto and usually the most awkward. So we split XGo into two layers: a cold vault for savings and an everyday wallet with a card. Moving money between them is a deliberate step with Face ID and a PIN.

Layer 1

Cold vault

04Wallet ID

A name instead of a 42 character address

Every user claims a readable Wallet ID like alice.xgo.id. One name covers every chain, works in 100+ wallets, apps and browsers, and makes sending money as simple as sending a message.

It also became the growth engine. People claimed their ID, shared it and asked friends to send money to it. Most of the 5K+ downloads after launch came this way, not from paid campaigns.

05Onboarding

Show the card before the seed phrase

The first version started like every other wallet: write down twelve words, confirm them, and only then see the product. We tested it against a flow that shows the value first and teaches security along the way. The seed phrase is still saved, just at the moment it starts to matter.

10Reflection

What I would do differently

I would put the vault in front of real users with real money much earlier. People judge safety by how a product feels when their own savings are on the line, and no prototype can fake that.

I would also bring marketing into research from the very first interview. The Wallet ID turned out to be the core of the growth story, and the launch could have been built around it even sooner.
